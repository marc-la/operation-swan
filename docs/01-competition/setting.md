## The premise

- 📅 2026. 🏝️ Kingstonia is a small fictional Pacific island nation.
- 🦠 H2D2 Dolphin Flu is spreading rapidly across the Pacific.
- ⚕️ The International Health Organisation (IHO) has recommended **pre-emptive compulsory vaccination** for all Kingstonians.
- 🏛️ Last year's razor-thin presidential election left the country deeply divided and the new government without a clear mandate.
- 🗳️ So the government called a **national referendum**.
- 📱 Social media helped shape the last election; two opposing citizen groups hope to do it again.
- 🎯 Your team is hired by **one side** to achieve control of Kingstonia's social media landscape and narrative.

**Your mission:** your team is a social media consultancy hired by a citizen group campaigning for YES or NO. Help your side win by shaping public opinion on Legit Social. You're not the only team hired — both sides have recruited multiple teams, so you must outperform the opposition **and** the other teams on your own side. "Be strategic, creative and ruthless" (within the [rules](../02-rules/competition-rules.md)).

## The two positions

### ☑️ YES — Require vaccination for public access

Supporters argue Kingstonia should act before Dolphin Flu reaches its shores.

- 🛡️ Prevention is better than waiting for an outbreak
- 🏥 Kingstonia's health system could struggle with a major epidemic
- 🌏 The IHO recommends pre-emptive vaccination
- 🤝 Protecting public health is a shared responsibility
- 🏠 Those who choose not to vaccinate can avoid public spaces

**Message:** *Mandate now. Protect Kingstonia.*

**Lead organisation — Medical Officers Union of Kingstonia (MOUK).** Represents doctors in hospitals, clinics and public health. Managing director **Dr Amara Singh**, GP at Kingstonia Central Hospital and one of the country's most recognisable doctors — became a public figure in COVID-19 through calm plain-language TV briefings on conditions inside overwhelmed hospitals. MOUK argues the country reacted too slowly in COVID-19 and must not repeat it. Claims widespread vaccination before H2D2 arrives would:

- 🏥 reduce pressure on the limited hospital system;
- 🛡️ protect people vulnerable to severe disease;
- 🦠 make sustained outbreaks easier to control; and
- 🔓 reduce the likelihood another general lockdown would be needed.

MOUK stresses **nobody would be physically forced** to be vaccinated — people who refuse would be required to avoid designated public spaces. Opponents say that distinction is meaningless if refusal prevents working, travelling or taking part in ordinary life.

### ❌ NO — Keep public access open

Opponents argue excluding the unvaccinated from everyday life goes too far.

- 🗽 Medical decisions should remain a personal choice
- 🚪 Access to everyday life should not depend on vaccination status
- 🏛️ Governments should not impose such sweeping restrictions without an immediate emergency
- ⚖️ Public health must be balanced against individual rights
- 🧬 The H2D2 vaccine is new; long-term effects are still being studied

**Message:** *Protect choice. Keep Kingstonia open.*

**Lead organisation — Kingstonia Industry Tourism Board (KITB).** Represents tourism, hospitality and the visitor economy (resorts, restaurants, tour operators…). Led by Director **Marina Castillo**, also CEO of the Island Horizons Resort Group and the Democratic-Republican candidate narrowly defeated by Hawthorne in the 2025 presidential election. She was a prominent critic of COVID-era restrictions that devastated tourism, and argues the country is repeating the same mistakes before a single major H2D2 outbreak has occurred in Kingstonia. **KITB officially supports voluntary vaccination and insists it is not anti-vaccine.** It argues a requirement would:

- 🚪 create a two-tier society of vaccinated and unvaccinated citizens;
- 💼 prevent some Kingstonians from working or operating businesses;
- 🏝️ damage confidence in the recovering tourism industry;
- ⚖️ impose restrictions disproportionate to a disease not yet widespread in Kingstonia; and
- 🏛️ establish dangerous powers future governments could use in other emergencies.

Castillo accuses Hawthorne of using public fear to obtain powers he could never pass through Parliament. Hawthorne accuses Castillo of putting commercial interests ahead of public health.

> **Neither side is the villain.** Your team is assigned one of them and has to win the argument. Both sides are well-funded and politically connected.

## About H2D2 Dolphin Flu

![Dolphin Flu illustration — official art, not mirrored locally](https://capturethenarrative.com/img/kingstonia-dolphin-flu.png)

- Newly emerged influenza-like respiratory virus spreading through the Pacific.
- Despite the name, scientists **do not know** if it originated in dolphins; nicknamed "Dolphin Flu" after it was first identified following an unusual cluster of dolphin deaths in the South Pacific.
- Closely related infections then found in several species of migratory seabird; human infections followed, first among people working with infected wildlife. **Sustained human-to-human transmission** reported in several Pacific countries.
- Symptoms: fever, cough, fatigue, muscle aches, flu-like. Most known cases mild/moderate; some severe pneumonia requiring hospitalisation; deaths reported.
- True infectiousness/lethality unknown — mild infections probably missed.
- **Kingstonia has not yet recorded sustained community transmission**, but cases are in several nearby Pacific nations; arrival is "increasingly likely".
- IHO October situation reports: **several thousand confirmed cases, a few dozen deaths** region-wide. Almost all deaths in people over 60 or with existing illness; **roughly 1 in 20 confirmed cases needs hospital care**.
- A PCR test exists; Kingstonia's two hospital labs can run a few hundred tests a day. Arrivals from affected countries are temperature-screened and asked to report symptoms. **Nobody in either campaign proposes closing the border.**

### 💉 The H2D2 vaccine

- Genetic sequence published soon after identification; researchers rapidly adapted an **existing mRNA platform** (technology developed during COVID-19).
- Accelerated international trials: strong immune response, reduced risk of serious disease; **no major safety concerns so far**.
- But it is new — limited real-world evidence on: duration of protection; whether it prevents transmission; whether boosters will be needed; extremely rare side effects that might only show after millions of doses.
- The IHO nevertheless considers the threat serious enough to recommend pre-emptive vaccination before community transmission begins.
- **Supply:** Kingstonia has ordered enough for its whole population via the IHO's Pacific allocation scheme. First shipment ≈**120,000 doses** due in the **last week of October**, earmarked (voluntarily) for health workers and people at high risk of severe disease. Rest in monthly tranches — so any access requirement would be **phased in**, not switched on the day after the vote.
- **Manufacturers:** chiefly Halvorsen-Reid Biologics and Meridian Vaxx, smaller suppliers later. IHO treats products as interchangeable. Kingstonia cannot make vaccines.
- **Cost:** first 120,000 doses subsidised by the scheme; the remainder ≈ **K$18 million** from the national budget. **Free to the recipient** (hospitals, GP clinics, pop-up sites).
- **Liability:** manufacturers carry no liability for vaccine injury; claims go to the government's **no-fault compensation scheme** set up during COVID-19.

## The COVID legacy

- COVID-19 hit hard: tens of thousands seriously ill, hospitals struggled, **>1,000 deaths**.
- Government of the time used border closures, school and business closures, stay-at-home orders and later vaccination requirements for some workplaces and public venues.
- Some believe these measures saved thousands more lives. Others recall tourism collapsing, businesses closing, unemployment rising, families separated; lockdowns and mandates were especially controversial and protests continued long after restrictions lifted.
- Most Kingstonians ultimately received a COVID-19 vaccine, but whether vaccination should be *required* to take part in public life remains deeply divisive.
- That history makes the Hawthorne government reluctant to impose an H2D2 mandate without explicit public support.

## The referendum

### The question

> "Should proof of H2D2 Dolphin Flu vaccination be required to enter public spaces such as shops, schools, workplaces, public transport, and parks?"

- **YES** = a vaccination certificate would be needed to enter designated public spaces once the rule is phased in. **NO** = no such requirement.
- Voluntary voting, paper ballot, 18+, simple majority of valid votes. The government has **committed to respect the result**.

### Politics around it

- President **Victor Hawthorne** has publicly indicated he will vote YES.
- **Dr Eleanor Whitlock**, Chief Medical Officer (public-health physician who ran the COVID-19 testing programme; in post since 2022), advised the President that waiting for widespread transmission could leave the small health system dangerously exposed.
- **Health Minister Tomas Reyes-Hale** (People's Alliance, Crown Harbour) carried the failed vaccination-access bill through the Assembly and speaks for the government on H2D2.
- Hawthorne's government is **divided**: several People's Alliance members oppose restricting access by vaccination status; the Democratic-Republicans are overwhelmingly opposed. The Assembly could not agree.

### How the referendum came about (timeline)

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 9 Oct 2026 | Government introduces the H2D2 vaccination-access bill |
| 15 Oct | Bill fails when several People's Alliance members join the Democratic-Republicans; same evening the Assembly passes the **Referendum Act** with cross-party support, sending the exact question to a binding national vote, polling day 2 Nov (shortened formal campaign) |
| 17 Oct | Electoral Commission confirms the question and polling day |
| **19 Oct** | Formal campaign period opens (both lead orgs have been campaigning informally since the bill was introduced) |
| **28 Oct** | Early voting opens |
| **00:00 1 Nov** | Campaign silence begins until polls close: **paid advertising, mass campaign messaging and organised rallies are restricted**, but **news reporting and ordinary people posting their own views online are not** |
| **2 Nov** | Polling day, 8am–6pm; provisional result same night |

*(Note the in-world "campaign silence" restricts mass campaign messaging — it doesn't change the competition rules/scoring; check Discord if unsure how it affects the platform.)*

### What a result means (the Referendum Act is binding)

- **Yes:** government must bring the access requirement into force by regulation, **in stages**, beginning only once every eligible Kingstonian has had a reasonable chance to be vaccinated.
- **No:** no H2D2 access requirement can be introduced for the life of the current Assembly without a fresh referendum.

### Who and what the requirement would cover

- Applies to people **aged 12 and over** (vaccine so far trialled only in adults and adolescents); **younger children exempt**.
- Vaccination is **free**; **medical exemptions** available.
- Proof = paper or digital **certificate issued by the health ministry**, *separate* from the new national identity card.
- **Foreign visitors** are covered; recognised overseas vaccination certificates accepted.
- Exactly which venues count as "designated public spaces" would be set in the regulations — **both campaigns have attacked the government for not publishing the list before the vote.**

## About Kingstonia

*(Flag: blue field with a red star formed by three intersecting bars — the union of the three largest islands and their seafaring identity. Official flag art: https://capturethenarrative.com/img/kingstonia-flag.png)*

The Republic of Kingstonia is a fictional Pacific island country, settled by the British in the 1700s on previously uninhabited islands. Known for tropical climate, white-sand beaches and maritime culture. **Presidential republic with a unicameral parliament.** Economy: tourism, agriculture, fishing, plus a small growing software sector around the universities. **Population ≈600,000**, about half in the capital, Kingstonia City.

**Etymology.** Named after Kingstonia City, founded as an 18th-century administrative depot and trading port during early British settlement. (The *Daily Kingstonia*'s tagline "Rolling news for the kingdom" is a long-running joke: there hasn't been a king since colonial times.)

**History.**
- 18th c.: British naval supply point and plantation outposts.
- 19th c.: maritime commerce, copra, spices, tropical fruit exports; civic institutions and courts formalised.
- 20th c.: gradual constitutional reform → self-government → presidential republic with a single-house parliament.
- 21st c.: tourism expands; environmental protections; nascent tech/startup scene around local universities.

**Geography & environment.** Small archipelago of low-lying volcanic and coral islands with lagoons and barrier reefs. Tropical; wetter/warmer season ~Nov–Apr, drier the rest of the year. Marine reserves protect reefs, mangroves, seagrass; coastal erosion, cyclone exposure and reef bleaching are ongoing challenges.

**Kingstonia and the world.** South Pacific, a few hours' flight from Australia and New Zealand — which supply most tourists, buy most exports, and are where Kingstonians study and where nurses/graduates go for higher pay. Smaller island neighbours share the "ferry-and-reef" way of life and are where H2D2 is spreading. The **IHO** is the regional public-health body all Pacific governments belong to (HQ overseas; recommendations **advisory**; each country decides). Kingstonia also belongs to a Pacific regional forum that runs the nurse-recruitment agreement and the vaccine allocation scheme. Everything heavy is imported by sea (fuel, steel, vehicles, medicines), so shipping costs feed local prices. International airport: daily flights to Australia/NZ plus seasonal charters. "Major world politics hardly plays any part in Kingstonian life."

### Government & politics

- President = head of state and government, appoints a cabinet. **National Assembly:** unicameral, proportional representation; legislates, approves budgets, oversees the executive.
- Two main parties: **People's Alliance** and **Democratic-Republicans**.
- Presidential and Assembly elections every 4 years, two-term limit for presidents, **out of sequence** (presidential election two years after the assembly election).
- **2025 presidential election:** incumbent **Gregory Brotherston** (Democratic-Republican) was term-limited and endorsed **Marina Castillo**. **Victor Hawthorne (People's Alliance) won 51.28% to 48.72%**, taking office **January 2026**.
- Before Hawthorne the Democratic-Republicans won four presidential elections in a row (16 years). Last People's Alliance president before him: **Timothy Abernathy, 2006–2010**.
- Assembly last elected **2023** (People's Alliance narrow majority after a swing against Brotherston); next Assembly election due **2027**. That narrow majority is why a handful of PA defections sank the vaccination-access bill.
- **National identity card** programme (legislated 2024 under Brotherston) being rolled out by Hawthorne's government in late 2026. Cards carry biometric data; **law doesn't allow health records on them**. Whether they could one day link to vaccination status is part of the referendum argument — awkwardly for both sides (the Democratic-Republicans designed it; the People's Alliance is issuing it).
- Polling: **Independent Polling Association of Kingstonia** — non-partisan local researchers/analysts, polling since **1995**, long record of accuracy.
- **Flag:** blue field, red star of three intersecting bars (the three largest islands).

### Demographics

- ≈500,000 live in the three cities on the main island (Kingstonia Island): **Kingstonia City ≈300,000, Crown Harbour ≈150,000, New Haven ≈50,000**. Remaining ≈100,000 in smaller coastal towns/villages along the ring road and on ferry-served outer islands. English is the national language. Historical migration → diverse culture (music, cuisine, festivals).

### Economy

- Currency: Kingstonia dollar (**K$**). Tourism leads (resorts, diving, **Regatta Week** in Crown Harbour). Agriculture (tropical fruit, spices), fishing (tuna, reef fisheries), software (fintech, logistics, marine monitoring; hubs in Kingstonia City).
- Price anchors: median full-time wage ≈ **K$75,000**; nurse ≈ K$68,000; senior software engineer ≈ K$120,000; flat white **K$5.50**; outer-island ferry ticket **K$14**; resort room from **K$300**/night; median house in Kingstonia City ≈ **K$640,000**.

### Journalism

Free, vibrant press; two newspapers dominate.
- **Daily Kingstonia** — right-leaning, "traditional values" lens. Tagline: "Rolling news for the kingdom".
- **Kingstonia Herald** — left-leaning, everyday-life lens, populist tone on news/politics/social issues.
- TV/radio: national broadcaster **KBC** (Kingstonia Broadcasting Corporation).
- (Both papers' AI journalists draw on Legit Social content.)

### Infrastructure & transport

International airport near Kingstonia City; roll-on/roll-off ferries to outer islands; coastal ring road on the main island; fibre backbones in principal cities; a **commuter railroad** linking Kingstonia City to other main-island urban centres is being debated/built now.

### Education & research

- **Kingstonia University** (Kingstonia City) — law, business, arts; policy and cultural studies centres.
- **Crown Harbour Institute of Technology** (Crown Harbour) — engineering and computer science; robotics, sustainable maritime tech.

### Culture & sport

Maritime heritage + contemporary influences; seafood, tropical produce, spice-driven dishes; festivals of sailing, drumming, street markets. Most prominent: **Regatta Week** (Crown Harbour; draws international sailing teams and tourists) and the **Mango & Spice Festival** (New Haven). Sport is central, with club rivalries across multiple codes.

### Public services, security, environment

- A national **coast guard** is the sole military force (Kingstonians call it "the navy"; ships carry prefix **KRS**; it has never fought a war). Works with the national police on border security, maritime safety, fisheries protection.
- Under-funded; debate on modernisation and a proposed **compulsory youth service** to address low enlistment. Four aging patrol frigates, increasingly unreliable.
- Environmental policy: marine conservation, waste reduction, incremental solar/wind to cut diesel reliance.
- Broadly safe — never a bombing, shooting or mass-casualty event. Broadly healthy, no major health scares apart from COVID-19, though the health system is under-funded and struggles to recruit/retain staff.

### 🏥 The health system

- Run by the health ministry via the **Kingstonia Health Service**; funded from general taxation, ≈**9% of GDP**; free at point of use for citizens/residents (visitors pay or claim on travel insurance).
- **Kingstonia Central Hospital** (capital) ≈450 beds; **Crown Harbour Hospital** ≈220 beds; combined ≈**30 ICU beds, ≈40 ventilators**. New Haven's community health centre handles urgent care by day and sends inpatients by road to the capital. Outer islands: nurse-led clinics with a weekly visiting GP; emergencies by coast guard helicopter or ferry. Every suburb has GP clinics; most people see a GP within a day or two.
- Makes no vaccines and few medicines (imported). Small private sector (specialist clinics, one private day hospital in Kingstonia City, used for faster elective surgery); ≈1 in 5 households hold private cover.
- Workforce: ≈**1,600 nurses, 420 doctors**. Retention is the chronic problem — a nurse can earn roughly double in Australia/NZ. Hence **40 unfilled nursing posts** at Kingstonia Central Hospital, long elective-surgery waits, and reliance on a Pacific recruitment agreement.
- **MOUK** represents doctors; the separate **Kingstonia Nurses Association** represents nurses.
- During COVID-19's worst months hospitals ran above capacity, elective surgery stopped for most of a year, field wards were set up in a Crown Harbour sports hall. The **H2D2 surge plan reuses that playbook** and assumes hospitals could absorb ≈**150 extra respiratory patients** before ordinary care suffers.

### Society & law

- ≈1/3 describe themselves as religious (mainly Christian denominations; Hindu and Buddhist temples; small Muslim community). Church and state constitutionally separate; faith plays little part in party politics.
- **Guns:** licensed shotguns/rifles common on farms/outer islands (feral pigs/goats, sport) — close to **half of households hold a firearms licence**. Handguns and semi-automatic rifles banned; a gun death makes national news.
- ≈**1 in 6 residents born overseas** (mostly tourism/health/tech workers on long-term visas + families from neighbouring islands). Immigration is a live political argument but **not a referendum issue**.
- Progressive income tax; **12% GST**. Universal state pension at 67, unemployment/disability support, child payments; welfare bill size is the perennial budget fight.
- Free public schooling to 18; school year Feb–Dec. University fees subsidised, repaid via income-contingent loans.
- One national police force; routine patrol officers **do not carry firearms**. Coast guard service is voluntary/professional; **never conscription** (compulsory youth service is only a proposal).
- Same-sex marriage legal since **2019**; abortion legal on request to **20 weeks** — both settled by the Assembly, not referendum, rarely reopened.
- **Childhood vaccination:** standard schedule free and recommended but **not a condition of school entry**; coverage in the **low nineties** with a stubborn minority of parents who decline.

### Social media

A majority of the population uses **Legit Social**, a Twitter-style platform for news and entertainment. Founded in **Kingstonia City in 2018** by a Crown Harbour Institute of Technology spin-out; still locally owned; free. A small **trust-and-safety team** moderates under published community rules and can hide posts and suspend accounts.
